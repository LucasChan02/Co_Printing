#### Reviewer #1 Comments

1. The abstract is clear but lacks a strong statement of novelty and  quantitative findings; it would be improved by explicitly stating the  key contribution and including a specific result (e.g., the critical  roughness or layer height affecting connectivity).
2. Overall, the  novelty is moderate. While the topic is relevant, AJP on rough or AM  substrates has been studied before. The paper mainly provides a  systematic experimental study rather than a fundamentally new approach.  The authors should better highlight what is new compared to existing  studies.
3. A major limitation is the absence of electrical  measurements. Since the work focuses on printed electronics and  connectivity, relying solely on visual continuity is insufficient. Even  basic resistance measurements would significantly strengthen the study.
4. The definition of “connectivity” is qualitative (? / ×). A more  quantitative evaluation (e.g., resistance or continuity metrics) would  enhance the analysis's rigor.
5. Surface roughness is controlled via  layer height, but standard roughness parameters (e.g., Ra, Rz) are not  reported. This makes it difficult to generalize the results or compare  with other studies.
6. The theory section is quite general and does  not directly support the experimental results. Including more  quantitative reasoning would strengthen the discussion.
7. The range  of process parameters explored is limited. A more systematic variation  of parameters would provide stronger conclusions.
8. The conclusion  mainly summarizes observations. It would be more useful to include  practical guidelines for process planning or roughness limits.



#### Reviewer #2 Comments

This manuscript investigates the role of substrate surface roughness on  aerosol jet printing (AJP) performance, with a focus on line continuity  and morphology. The topic is relevant to hybrid additive manufacturing  and printed electronics, and the study provides useful experimental  observations. The paper is generally well organized, and the use of  controlled FDM substrates to systematically vary roughness is a  strength. However, several aspects need clarification and further  development before the work can be considered for publication.

1. The study uses layer height as a proxy for surface roughness, but no direct roughness metrics (e.g., Ra, Rz) are reported. This makes it difficult  to generalize the findings or compare them with other studies. Including quantitative roughness measurements would significantly strengthen the  work and allow for more rigorous interpretation.

2. The manuscript  focuses on morphological connectivity without measuring electrical  performance. While the authors justify this due to low sintering  temperature, the lack of electrical data limits the practical impact of  the study. Even simple resistance measurements would provide important  validation of the observed “connected vs disconnected” behavior.  Sometime the line is connected under the microscope, but the resistance  could still be very high or even the electrically disconnected. 

   For  all electronic devices printed with the AJP, electrical conductivity is  very important. The are several ways that could help solve the problem.  1. High T resin can be chosen for the FDM printing. 2. Check the  roughness of the surface first then do printing and sintering. The  heating process will not impact the printing process. 3. Choose ink  which does not need heating such as conductive polymer ink (PEDOT PSS).

3. The explanation of particle deposition relies mainly on qualitative  reasoning (e.g., insufficient particle delivery in grooves, capillary  flow). No modeling or quantitative framework is provided to support  these claims.  Since the paper mentions CFD as future work, including at least a simplified analysis or scaling argument would improve the  scientific depth.
4. The manuscript shows that higher deposition rate improves continuity but introduces width variability and material  accumulation in grooves (Fig. 5). However, this trade-off is only  qualitatively described. A more systematic comparison (e.g., plotting  line width variation vs deposition rate) would make this conclusion more convincing.
5. The criteria used to define “connected” vs  “disconnected” lines (Table 2) are not clearly stated. A quantitative or at least semi-quantitative definition would be helpful.
6. At  present, the transition from connected to disconnected lines (e.g.,  around the 0.3–0.4 mm layer height range) appears empirical and somewhat case-specific. The authors are encouraged to go beyond descriptive  results and provide a more systematic or predictive framework. For  example, it would be valuable to analyze: The relationship between  surface roughness (or groove depth) and particle deposition per unit  area. Critical threshold condition (e.g., ratio of deposited material  volume to surface area increase) that leads to disconnection. The  combined effects of process parameters (print speed, standoff distance,  gas flow rate) in defining a “safe printing window”. Any of these  conclusions can be very beneficial to future research.
